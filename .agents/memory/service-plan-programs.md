---
name: Seasonal service-plan programs
description: User rules for finite visit ranges, mid-season enrollment, service crews, capacity overrides and advance notices.
---

A plan may use first and last service dates plus a total count that includes both endpoints. Keep endpoint dates exact; evenly distribute the interior visits and use the nearest chosen weekday. Weeks are the default unit, with days available.

**Why:** The user wants seasonal treatment schedules rather than manually spacing every visit.

**How to apply:** Do not silently drop visits when the chosen date range cannot fit them. Ask for another range, count or weekday.

Mid-season enrollment may either have independent dates or join the remaining rounds of an existing plan of the same template. Alignment continues the source treatment sequence, rather than restarting at the first treatment. Joining its route is an explicit choice.

**Why:** New customers may need to join an established lawn or pest-treatment program during the year.

**How to apply:** Preserve the enrolled schedule and avoid creating replacement visits merely because an existing visit was moved or cancelled.

Each round may have different services or several services together. Use one crew by default, with optional per-service crews and chemical-application records.

**Why:** The user specifically wants fertilizer and sprinkler crews to work from one plan when needed.

**How to apply:** Keep service attribution when recording chemicals, and make any required-record choice explicit to staff.

Warn and normally block over-capacity scheduling, but allow an owner or authorized manager to explicitly override with a reason and retain the requested day/time.

**Why:** The user changed the initial hard-block choice to allow intentional over-capacity bookings.

**How to apply:** Record who approved the override. Include other work and travel planning in workload checks; never silently approve overflow or overwrite an appointment with an optimized route ETA.

Offer advance email, text and customer push notices, respecting company delivery setup and customer permissions.

**Why:** Customers need time to prepare their property for the visit.

**How to apply:** Do not equate staff mobile push registration with customer notification consent.

Finite service dates do not by themselves change billing cadence, auto-renewal or agreed prices, and billing renewal must not repeat a finished finite visit schedule.

**Why:** A service-scheduling change is not permission to change billing or create extra work.

**How to apply:** Keep visit planning separate from the existing recurring-billing agreement.
